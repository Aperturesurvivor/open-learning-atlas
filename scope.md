# MVP scope: teacher-facing personalized planning demonstration

## Goal

Test whether Open Learning Atlas can support a teacher in planning a next math lesson for an individual student. Use one small mathematics topic, a transparent, deterministic rule-based recommendation, and an editable lesson/activity draft. The teacher remains in control.

## Intended user and problem

Explore the planning workload of educators, including special education teachers. The specific workflow and unmet need are hypotheses to validate with educators, not established claims.

## MVP workflow

1. Use a small, clearly identified subset of the atlas; distinguish candidate records from independently reviewed material.
2. Accept a synthetic student profile and problem-level results; let a teacher provide context and corrections.
3. Apply explicit, inspectable rules to suggest a next concept or review activity, showing the evidence, uncertainty, and atlas relationships used. Do not infer a definitive diagnosis or mastery from a single answer.
4. Produce a short editable lesson/activity draft for teacher review; the teacher can revise or reject it.
5. Demonstrate the workflow using fictional data and document its limitations and results. Decide the eventual portfolio presentation based on actual outcomes.

## Boundaries

The atlas remains a shared knowledge map. Student records, recommendations, lessons, and assessments belong in a separate downstream demonstration or overlay; they do not become canonical atlas content. No full adaptive textbook, public curriculum scoring, clinical/educational placement decisions, real student data, or claims of validated educational efficacy are required for this MVP. Existing mathematics v1 completion and human-review gates remain unchanged.

## Evidence and validation

Define test examples covering the same inputs producing the same rule-based suggestion, missing information, teacher overrides, and disagreement between evidence sources. Have an educator review whether the draft is useful and whether it saves meaningful preparation time; revise the hypothesis based on findings. Review subject content and disclose unreviewed atlas material.

## Existing alternatives

[i-Ready](https://www.curriculumassociates.com/programs/i-ready-learning/personalized-instruction) describes assessment-informed lesson paths and teacher-assigned lessons. [Khanmigo](https://www.khanacademy.org/khanmigo) describes lesson planning and student-work summaries. Their existence does not establish whether they meet the particular needs of special education teachers; compare their current capabilities and investigate educator workflows before asserting a market gap.
