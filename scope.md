# Scope: adaptive worksheet demonstration

## Goal

Demonstrate how Open Learning Atlas could support teachers, including special education teachers, in creating and adapting math worksheets for an individual student. This is a downstream prototype using the Atlas, not a change to the Atlas's canonical purpose.

## MVP loop

1. A teacher sets a starting grade or skill level across a small set of math skills, plus an optional current focus.
2. The system generates an editable worksheet covering the selected skills.
3. The teacher records results for individual problems.
4. Explicit, inspectable rules use those results to adjust the next worksheet; show the reason for adjustments and allow teacher override.
5. Demonstrate the full loop with fictional student data and document results and limitations.

Use a small identified Atlas subset and distinguish candidate records from reviewed records. The teacher reviews problems and answers. Wrong answers alone do not establish why a learner struggled or prove mastery. The student profile and worksheet records are a downstream overlay, not canonical Atlas content. Website versus device app and the repository for the implementation remain to be decided.

## Boundaries and evaluation

Use fictional data initially. No promise of validated learning outcomes, diagnostic or placement decisions, complete adaptive curriculum, or public benchmarking. Evaluate determinism of the adaptation rules, correctness of generated problems and answers, teacher overrides, and usefulness through educator feedback. Existing Atlas mathematics v1 review and completion requirements remain unchanged. Decide the portfolio artifact after there is concrete work to present.

## Prior art

[i-Ready](https://www.curriculumassociates.com/programs/i-ready-learning/personalized-instruction) offers assessment-informed lesson paths, while [Khanmigo](https://www.khanacademy.org/khanmigo) offers teacher planning tools. Whether existing tools address the particular worksheet-planning needs of special education teachers remains a research question. MasteryMath is a related earlier proof of concept worth locating and reviewing.
