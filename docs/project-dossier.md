# Public Project Dossier

## Identity and Status

Open Learning Atlas is an open-data and open-source educational knowledge-map
project initiated by Josiah Wilson in 2026. It is currently a working public-
alpha dataset and architecture, not a completed educational standard or a
validated claim of learner outcomes.

## Problem and Intended Benefit

Courses, standards, applications, and assessments often describe overlapping
knowledge using incompatible labels and coarse sequences. The project aims to
provide stable, reviewable coordinates that those systems can reference while
remaining free to choose different routes, thresholds, and teaching methods.

Intended beneficiaries include learners, educators, curriculum designers,
institutions, researchers, AI-system builders, and authors of educational tools.
No downstream impact has yet been demonstrated at institutional scale.

## What Exists

The repository currently includes a constitution, data model, JSON Schema,
semantic validator, deterministic compiler, 1,210-node connected mathematics
alpha with 2,937 typed relationships, territory coverage notes, checksummed
release package, dependency-free query interface, downstream conformance
fixtures, and a responsive interactive viewer. All 85 current territories pass
the measurable breadth floor. Qualitative mathematical and educational review
needed for v1 remains open.

## Origin and Attribution

Josiah Wilson originated the project direction: an OpenStreetMap-like map that
could support project-derived and personalized learning without embedding one
curriculum. OpenAI Codex has provided substantial architectural analysis,
research assistance, drafting, data generation, validation, and implementation.
AI-generated claims begin as candidates and are not represented as independent
expert review.

MSC2020 was used as an external breadth checklist. Its codes, hierarchy, and
classification text were not imported. Additional sources remain subject to
their own rights and are cited rather than relicensed.

## Risks and Limitations

- An apparently precise graph can conceal disputed dependencies or cultural and
  pedagogical assumptions.
- AI can create plausible but incorrect definitions, omissions, or rationales.
- Institutions might misuse the map as a mandatory sequence or surveillance
  framework despite the base-map boundary.
- Coverage may overrepresent English-language and Western educational sources.
- A visual map may suggest completeness where the open-world dataset remains
  provisional.

The project mitigates these risks through candidate status, attributable review,
explicit confidence and conditions, version history, open disagreement, and a
strict separation between shared terrain and private learner state.

## Success Evidence

The current evidence is structural and functional: validators pass; a clean
checkout reproduces the dataset, package, and viewer; and both humans and agents
can search, resolve, traverse, inspect, and audit typed claims. The model can
represent conditional alternatives and downstream coverage/gap operations
without storing a curriculum or learner. Claims about improved learning,
assessment accuracy, or institutional usefulness remain hypotheses until
independently evaluated.
